## 5. Decisive attention experiment (2026-10-26, user-approved; `artifacts/eval/attention_decisive.json`)

Pre-registered single-variable follow-up closing the two loopholes of the R2 negative result: (a) implementation identity — HSTU re-implemented with official `meta-recsys/generative-recommenders` STULayer semantics (silu-U-only, input position/time embeddings, 3x output concat, eps 1e-6), parity vs an independent dense transcription of the official ops **max_abs_diff 1.1e-06** (`scripts/hstu_alignment_check.py`); (b) sequence length — max_seq_len 50 → 200. All else byte-identical to the frozen protocol (SS256, 8 epochs, seed 42, same 40k frozen sample, same-window LightGBM baselines retrained in-run). H&M only (user decision; swire lacks a same-run frozen baseline).

| MAP@12 (n=2,667 paired buyers) | MeanPool | GRU | SASRec | HSTU-aligned | B1 GBDT |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L=50 | 0.0348 | 0.0343 | 0.0339 | **0.0176** | 0.0404 |
| L=200 | 0.0345 | **0.0355** | 0.0342 | 0.0209 | 0.0404 |

- **MeanPool/GRU/SASRec at L=50 exactly replicate** the confirmation run (0.0348/0.0343/0.0339) — pipeline determinism check passed.
- **Attention vs order-free pooling: no supported gain at either length.** SASRec−MeanPool at L=200 = −0.0002 (p=0.94), unchanged by length. GRU−MeanPool at L=200 = +0.0011 [−0.0020,+0.0042] (p=0.46) — the only point estimate in the whole study where a sequential encoder exceeds MeanPool, not significant, and the architecture is recurrent, not attention.
- **Length effects in absolute MAP are largest for HSTU-aligned (+0.0033), then GRU (+0.0012), SASRec (+0.0003), MeanPool (−0.0003)** — but HSTU-aligned remains substantially below both non-attention and GBDT baselines at both lengths. GRU−B1 narrows to −0.0049 [−0.0091,−0.0007] p=0.02, still significantly negative.
- **CRITICAL scope check — effective history lengths (GPT-required audit):** the sequence unit is the item event. Among the 39,261 evaluated customers with any history, only **697 (1.8%) have >50 events and 5 (0.013%) have >200** (S2: 630/6,729 and 5). The L=200 batch therefore leaves the input **byte-identical for 98%+ of customers** — it is a *maximum-context-configuration experiment*, not a demonstrated long-history-capability experiment, and GRU's +0.0012 cannot be attributed to newly added history. Additional non-equivalence: the official HSTU normalizes attention by the padded length (1/N), so short-history customers receive 4x smaller attention weights at L=200 than at L=50 (GRU/SASRec/MeanPool are input-equivalent across lengths).
- **Implementation identity resolved the other way**: the official-aligned HSTU is *worse* than V1 (0.0176 vs 0.0338 at L=50; −0.0172 vs MeanPool, p<0.001). Reported as-is per GPT: V1 cannot serve as a validated HSTU reference score, but the V1→aligned change bundles several internal modifications, so no single-cause attribution of the drop is made. Both HSTU variants sit below MeanPool under the frozen budget.
- **Verdict (GPT-signed wording):** ATTENTION ADVANTAGE CLAIM: **REJECTED UNDER THE TESTED PROTOCOL** — "本实验未支持 Attention 的增量价值"（not "attention has no value on this task"; absence of a detected significant advantage is not proof of exactly-zero effect; an equivalence-margin design would be required for that). Evidence scope: H&M frozen 40k sample, Top-5,000 universe, SS256, 8 epochs, seed 42, L=50/200, unified scoring task. GPT-approved §5 conclusion paragraph:

> 本研究针对 Attention 可能因早期实现偏差或上下文长度不足而被低估的假设，进行了预注册确认实验。在保持数据样本、负采样、训练预算与统一推荐目标不变的条件下，研究首先对 HSTU 核心前向数学进行参考对齐，然后将最大上下文长度由 50 扩展至 200。结果显示，SASRec 在两个长度配置下均未获得相对于无序 MeanPool 的可靠预测优势；经过数学对齐的 HSTU 小规模变体也明显低于 MeanPool 与强 GBDT。GRU 在 L=200 时取得了略高于 MeanPool 的 MAP@12 点估计，但配对置信区间跨零，未达到统计确认，而且 GRU 不使用 Attention。因此，本实验否定了"Attention 在当前冻结推荐任务中已经展示可验证预测优势"的研究主张。这一结论限定于 H&M 的固定客户样本、Top5000 商品宇宙、统一判别式推荐目标、单种子及既定训练预算，不构成对 HSTU 原生生成式架构、工业规模训练或其他序列推荐任务的普遍否定。

## 7. Retail-domain cross-check on Instacart (2026-10-27, user-directed; GPT verdict: APPROVE WITH REQUIRED REVISIONS — revisions applied below)

**Motivation.** §6 established that long histories (ML-1M, ~165 events/user, rating domain) let sequence models beat unordered pooling 2.8x. The open question: does that hold for **retail** purchase streams at the same density? Instacart (official competition mirror via Kaggle) is the test: flattened SKU events with TRUE order (order_number asc, add_to_cart_order asc — real within-basket ordering that H&M lacks), 5-core iterative filter, 20,000 users sampled seed 42. Post-filter density: mean 164.3 / median 91 events per user, 16.2 orders per user, 41,147-item catalog — a near-exact density match to ML-1M (165.6/96) with a different domain, catalog scale, and behavior generator.

**Task naming (GPT-required correction).** This is **Instacart flattened next-item prediction**, NOT a reproduction of the official next-basket recommendation task. Target-structure audit (below) shows 95.2% of LOO test targets share the basket with the last input event — the task is predominantly *within-basket* next-item prediction.

**Protocol.** Identical frozen encoder protocol to ML-1M v2: LOO (second-to-last = validation, last = test), full ranking over all items, no seen-masking, SS256+logq, d128/2L/4H/L200, AdamW 2e-3, 100-epoch cap with val-HR@10 early stopping, seed 42. Pre-registered reference baselines added for the retail domain: trivial **freq_item** (rank by user's historical frequency), **last_item** (rank by recency), global popularity; and LightGBM-5Count-Diagnostic under the same full-ranking protocol.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**Target-structure audit (GPT-required, no retraining; `scripts/diag_instacart_target_structure.py`).** Test targets: 100% in the user's last order; **95.2% share the basket with the last input event**; 63.6% sit in the official train-set order; median add-to-cart position 9 (mean 10.5) — the model sees the earlier items of the same basket. **Findings (GPT-signed scope).**
